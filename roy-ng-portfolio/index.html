Retail Banking · Mobile App

Redesigning the pre-login experience of a major HK banking app

The pre-login landing page is the front door of a bank — every customer passes through it. The legacy page buried the login behind cluttered promotions and confused first-time users. I led the redesign from research to developer handoff.

RoleLead UX/UI (Consultant)
TeamPM · PO · Devs · 2 Designers
TimelineResearch → Spec, 1 quarter
PlatformiOS & Android

01 · Problem

  • Low-value content dominated the screen — promotions crowded out the actions users actually came for.
  • Login and registration were hard to find, generating drop-off and support calls.
  • Interaction patterns were inconsistent, built over years by different vendors.

02 · Research & evidence

  • Benchmarked 13 banks across Mainland China, Hong Kong and overseas, scoring each on 4 dimensions: branding, functional, promotional, informative.
  • Key insight: Mainland banks optimise for function-density; HK and foreign banks lead with brand — but nearly all winners surface login + quick actions within thumb reach.
  • Ran stakeholder interviews to align business KPIs (promotion visibility) with user goals (fast login).

03 · Exploration

  • Created and iterated 8 design options, spanning "content-rich" to "radically simplified".
  • Selected direction: scrollable promotional banner, login/register in a fixed prominent position, most-used functions surfaced in quick access, and all pre-login functions reachable one-handed in the lower screen zone.
  • Every colour decision was validated against WCAG accessibility contrast requirements.

04 · Outcome

−40%*Fewer taps to reach login for returning users
8 → 1Design options iterated and converged with stakeholders
100%Screens delivered with dev-ready specs & marketing guidance

*Measured against the legacy flow in internal walkthroughs.

"Roy's competitor analysis gave us the confidence to remove content we'd been afraid to touch for years."

Banking · Identity & Onboarding

Digital ID verification: designing trust into remote account opening

Remote account opening lives or dies on one moment: scanning your ID and face. Users who fail here rarely come back. I designed the end-to-end eID&V flow — and validated it with guerrilla research before a line of code was written.

RoleLead UX/UI (Consultant)
Research4 virtual banks benchmarked · 5 usability participants
ScopeID scan · Face recognition · Onboarding
DeliverablesFlow map · Hi-fi wireframes · Spec

01 · Problem

  • ID + face scanning was a new behaviour for most users — high anxiety, low familiarity.
  • Environmental issues (poor lighting, glare, unstable connection) silently caused failures with no guidance to recover.

02 · Research & evidence

  • Benchmarked 4 virtual banks (Ant Bank, ZA Bank, WeLab, Mox): all used text guidance, few gave completion feedback per step — a clear gap to own.
  • Ran guerrilla usability tests with 5 participants using two flow variants (think-aloud, scenario-based).
  • Findings drove real changes: users wanted animated guidance over text, an instruction page before ID selection, password creation moved to the end, and reminders for flash / card preparation.

03 · Solution

  • "Point to note" preparation screen (do's & don'ts) so users fix lighting and card issues before starting, not after failing.
  • Step-by-step animated guidance during ID scan and face recognition, with explicit completion states so users always know where they are.
  • Onboarding tutorial that shows only once, skippable — helpful for first-timers, invisible for returners.

04 · Outcome

5/5Test participants completed the full verification flow unaided
2 flowsA/B flow variants tested before development — saving rework
0 → 1New verification pattern shipped into the bank's flow library
Design Ops · Design System

From fragmented UI to a single design system

The hardest problem in the banking engagement wasn't a screen — it was entropy. Years of vendor teams shipping without shared standards left the app with mismatched colours, buttons and type. A big-bang redesign was impossible, so I designed the escape route.

The approach

  • Conducted a full UI audit to map every inconsistency across the product.
  • Built a design system — unified tokens, components and usage guidelines — published as the team's shared Figma library.
  • Established a living process: every new component is contributed back, so consistency compounds instead of decaying.

Why it matters

This is the work that outlives any single feature. The system let multiple squads revamp the app function-by-function while converging on one visual language — without ever freezing delivery for a "grand redesign".
